
## The *Homeric Hymn to Aphrodite*

The *Homeric Hymn to Aphrodite* is an anonymous ancient Greek epic poem in dactylic hexameter, part of the collection known as the Homeric Hymns, composed likely in the late seventh or early sixth century BC. 

The poem describes one of the final instances of a goddess uniting with a mortal. Zeus, to humble Aphrodite after she caused other gods to love mortals, compels her to fall in love with the Trojan shepherd Anchises on Mount Ida. Disguised as a mortal maiden, Aphrodite approaches him, and Anchises, awestruck by her beauty, questions her divinity before she claims to be a Phrygian princess. They spend the night together, after which Aphrodite reveals her true identity to the terrified Anchises, warning him never to boast of the union lest Zeus punish him. She prophesies the birth of their son Aeneas, who will rule among the Trojans, marking the end of an era of frequent divine-mortal liaisons.
